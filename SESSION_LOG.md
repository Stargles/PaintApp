Session 56 — 2026-08-21: Closed `tmp/vec-undo` — **a vector layer's move/scale/rotate is undoable**, the BUGS.md entry three sessions had noticed and none had taken. `setVectorTransform` wrote the cel's `VectorCanvas.transform` and registered nothing, so Undo after a Move reached straight past it to whatever the artist did before. **The obvious fix is the wrong one and that is why it survived**: its call site `CanvasView.Coordinator.objectTransformChanged` fires on every touch-move, so a `recordUndo` there is hundreds of steps for one drag. **The bracket hangs off `isVectorTransforming`'s own `didSet`, on `CanvasManager`, not on the coordinator** — that is the whole design decision. Two paths clear that flag with *no* gesture ending (`rasterizeLayer`, and `handleActiveContextChanged` on a layer/frame switch); a coordinator-owned bracket needs both of them to remember to close it, and a bracket that leaks is worse than none, since it either drops the step or bills the artist's **next** drag to this one. Hung off the property observer, the flag cannot go false without the bracket closing, because going false *is* the close — `CanvasPresentationModifier`'s `.onDisappear`/`.onChange` interlock with the observer playing the modifier's part. **It opens on the gesture's first write, not when the flag turns on**, which buys three cases for free: a tap that moves nothing never opens one; an in-between cel is excluded without a second guard, because `objectTransformChanged` already refuses to write on one; and undo pressed mid-drag re-baselines, since `finalizePendingGesturesForHistoryAction` closes the bracket (the fill's and the shape's rule, which the transform needed too — there is no gesture end to hang a commit on, the artist stays in Move mode across an undo). **The captured cel is held by reference plus IDs, never by index**: both leak paths close *after* `currentLayerIndex` has moved or *after* the cel has dropped its `vector`, so a re-resolved index would name the wrong cel or none. **Ordering in `rasterizeLayer` is now load-bearing rather than tidy** — the clear stays above `withStructureUndo`, so the transform step is pushed before the rasterize step and Undo, popping last-first, walks back through the rasterize and only then the transform; the other order un-transforms a cel whose `vector` has not been put back. What the step stores is the `CGAffineTransform` on each side, and that is lossless because `setTransform` **replaces** rather than composes; redo is its mirror. **One cel, matching `setVectorTransform`** — a multi-cel vector layer transformed on two frames has genuinely had two `VectorCanvas` objects changed and wants two steps. The no-op guard compares with a tolerance rather than `==`, because the overlay round-trips through `layerTransform(pivot:)`/`affine(from:pivot:)` (atan2 out, sin/cos back), so even a bare tap on a handle hands back a value that is equal to the eye and need not be bit-equal — an `==` there bills a tap as a move. `canUndo` joins `fillGestureActive`/`shapeGestureActive` and asks whether the layer has actually *moved*, not merely whether a bracket is open. **Tests: 13 new**, `VectorTransformUndoLogicTests`, all in the fast tier; the two leak-path tests each carry a **follow-up gesture**, because "the step arrived" does not prove the baseline was cleared and only the second drag landing on its own start does. Session 60 — 2026-08-21: Closed `tmp/lassomove-spec` — **`LASSO_MOVE.md`, a spec and not a build**, TODO.md queued item (a), the largest of the owner's four lasso/move asks. No code changed and **no test run was taken, deliberately**: the branch is three markdown files and three other agents held `simlock.sh`'s slots. **The headline is that this is much smaller than it looks, and the citations are the deliverable.** Every hard part is already shipped for the vector eraser: `StrokeGeometry.splitRuns(_:inside:)` is *literally* a selection clip — its own doc says so, and `StrokeCanvasView` already uses it to stop a stroke drawn across a selection from bridging the gap — `VectorCanvas.cutAlongFootprint`/`cutToIntersection` are the splice template, `DabLattice` + `stampStroke(visibleRange:)` keep a cut piece's dabs bit-identical, and `registerVectorFillUndo` is the whole-array undo shape. **The gap is one branch**: `TopToolbar.toggleMove()` checks `activeLayerIsVector` before it looks at anything and never reads `canvasManager.selection`, so the lasso stays on screen while `setVectorTransform` moves the entire cel — exactly the report. **The two questions the brief called hardest both came back the other way.** *Fills are the easy half*: `VectorFillElement` is an archived `CGPath`, so a chunk is `CGPath.intersection`/`.subtracting` — Core Graphics, iOS 16+, against a 26.5 deployment target — and a standalone `swiftc -O` harness MEASURED that the results are **fill-rule normalised** (a split donut's halves render identically under winding and even-odd), that **area is conserved exactly** (2200 + 6200 = 8400), that a grazing loop returns `isEmpty` and a containing loop empties the other side (two free fast paths), and that the cost is **0.86 + 1.27 ms** for an 8000-point fill against a 1500-point loop. *Strokes are the fiddly half*, and the load-bearing find is the **dab lattice**: `VectorCanvas.stamp` renders a piece from `lattice?.samples ?? stroke.samples`, so translating a piece's samples and leaving its lattice alone draws it **where it used to be** — and the eraser's `piece.lattice = nil` is equally wrong here, because re-anchoring re-phases every dab, so the half the artist did *not* touch would visibly change. The rule is asymmetric: **the stationary piece keeps the parent's lattice verbatim; the moved piece keeps it with `lattice.samples` translated by the same delta**, `parameters` and `seedID` untouched — which makes a translation bit-exact and is one of the reasons stage 1 is translation-only. **Recommended centreline over ink**, on four grounds, the deciding one being that `cutToIntersection`'s own comment already states the same assumption and the owner accepted it; ink membership is a *signed distance to the polygon*, which `CGPath` does not expose at all, so it is a day's work behind a setting rather than a constant change. **`conservativeCuts` must NOT be reused** — its half-width inset exists to hide a cut under a retained punch, and a move has no punch, so insetting would open a visible gap on both sides. **Text moves whole iff its bounding-box centre is inside** — the stroke rule rounded to the nearest whole object; *intersects* drags a paragraph out of the page and *wholly contains* silently leaves a title behind. **Erase punches move like the strokes they are**, because a hole that stays put while its ink leaves *heals* and then `collectResidueGarbage()` deletes the orphan, making the healing permanent. **Undo is a session, not `b100d65`'s bracket** — that bracket stores two affines and its own doc says that suffices *because a transform touches nothing else in the cel*, which a display-list rewrite does; what is inherited is its lesson, close-however-it-ends, which `beginCanvasEdit()` already delivers for free. **One artefact named rather than tuned away**: `BrushStamper`'s boundary dab is inclusive, and a *move* split is the first case whose two ranges abut, so one dab is double-stamped at zero delta — invisible above opacity 1, pinned by a test. **Interpolation is out of scope and safe**: an in-between is already refused by `toggleMove`'s existing guard, and a split on a keyframe only demotes tier 0 to the point-cloud path, which the eraser already does. Staged strokes → fills → whole objects → follow-ups, each with named headless tests asserting identities. **A separate, reachable defect found while scoping and left alone**: `SelectPanel`'s Duplicate calls `beginDuplicate()` directly, which has no vector branch and rasterizes a lassoed region onto a new raster layer. **What needs the owner**: whether the centreline rule feels right on their actual line weights, and whether the loop should survive a move.
